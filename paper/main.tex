\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background} Response to CD19-directed chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy varies among patients with refractory or relapsed B-cell lymphoma. We evaluated whether the T-cell receptor (TCR) sequence repertoire could discriminate recorded responders from nonresponders when patients were held entirely out of model training.

\noindent\textbf{Methods} We used 239,634 eligible TCR beta-chain records from 34 patients, including 18 responders and 16 nonresponders. We crossed five stratified five-fold patient partitions with two training seeds for the DeepTCR sequence classifier. Separate three- and four-fold evaluations brought the total to 57 fits. A composition baseline used the same patient partitions.

\noindent\textbf{Results} The median within-fold patient AUROC across ten five-fold DeepTCR evaluations was \textbf{0.456} (range 0.298--0.579). The matched composition baseline had median AUROC 0.386 across five partitions. DeepTCR exceeded the matched baseline in eight of ten evaluations. AUROC was 0.438 in the three-fold and 0.611 in the four-fold sensitivity check.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions} The primary patient-held-out estimates do not establish consistent discrimination in this cohort. The observed variation across splits and training seeds limits interpretation of single-run performance.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords} T-cell receptor, DeepTCR, CD19 CAR-T, B-cell lymphoma, patient-held-out validation
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{CD19 CAR-T Cell Therapy and Clinical Challenge}

CD19-directed CAR-T products redirect autologous T cells to recognize CD19-expressing malignant B cells. Clinical studies have documented responses in refractory or relapsed B-cell lymphoma \citep{june2018cart,neelapu2017axicabtagene,schuster2019tisagenlecleucel}. Response rates and durability vary across patients and products. Tumor burden, inflammatory markers and product characteristics have been studied as predictors of response \citep{locke2020tumor,deng2020characteristics}.

\subsection{CAR-T Product T-Cell Receptor Repertoires}

CAR-T cells retain their endogenous T-cell receptors. The repertoire can reflect the clonal history of cells in the infused product, and studies have associated characteristics of infused T cells with treatment outcomes \citep{fraietta2018determinants,chen2021singlecell}. The present analysis uses beta-chain sequence records and archived patient response labels.

\subsection{T-Cell Receptor Biology and Repertoire Analysis}

The complementarity-determining region 3 (CDR3) is a variable part of the TCR that contributes to antigen recognition. The CDR3 sequence and the identities of its V and J genes provide complementary descriptions of receptor sequence diversity \citep{davis1988t,robins2009comprehensive,glanville2017identifying,dash2017quantifiable}. Repertoire summaries and sequence-based models offer different ways to represent these data.

\subsection{Sequence-Based Models for TCR Repertoire Classification}

DeepTCR provides sequence-level and repertoire-level models for TCR data \citep{sidhom2021deeptcr}. This study uses its sequence classifier and aggregates its output to the patient level. Each patient contributes thousands of records but only one recorded outcome. Keeping each repertoire intact in validation is necessary to assess performance on patients absent from model training.

\subsection{Study Objectives}

The primary objective was to estimate patient-held-out response discrimination for DeepTCR and measure its variability across patient partitions and training seeds. The secondary objectives were to compare a sequence-composition baseline on the same partitions and describe exploratory repertoire characteristics and cluster occupancy.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Study Design and Patient Cohort}

The retrospective trainee study included 34 patients with refractory or relapsed B-cell lymphoma treated with CD19 CAR-T therapy. We used the archived binary response labels. The original submission describes responders as patients who achieved complete or partial response at the day-90 assessment, and nonresponders as those with stable or progressive disease. The archived labels contain 18 responders and 16 nonresponders.

\subsection{TCR Sequence Data and Processing}

The input consisted of 239,637 processed sequence records with a patient identifier, CDR3 beta-chain amino-acid sequence, V and J labels, and a binary outcome. The DeepTCR 40-residue input limit excluded three records, leaving 239,634 records across all 34 patients. The archived label array was verified against the processed input and each patient had one consistent label.

\subsection{Feature Encoding and DeepTCR Architecture}

The model was the official DeepTCR\_SS 2.1.29 large convolutional sequence classifier with a two-class head. Inputs were CDR3 beta-chain amino-acid sequences and the recorded V and J labels. Constant alpha-chain placeholders satisfy the package interface and were identical for all records. The V and J vocabularies were fit within each training fold. A gene value absent from a training vocabulary was encoded as unknown in that fold.

\subsection{Training Configuration}

The models used Adam with learning rate 0.001 and batches of 1,024 sequences. Each fit trained for ten fixed epochs on all eligible records from the training patients. The protocol held model settings and epoch count fixed across the experiment. It did not use held-out outcomes to select an epoch or tune settings. The repeated runs used Python 3.9.18, TensorFlow 2.12.0 and the Apple Silicon CPU backend.

\subsection{Repeated Patient-Held-Out Cross-Validation}
\label{sec:cv}

The primary design crossed five stratified five-fold patient partitions with two training seeds, giving ten five-fold evaluations and 50 fits. The partition seeds were 20261121--20261125. The training seeds were 20261201 and 20261202. Partitioning operated on the 34 patient labels, so all records from a patient remained within one fold. Each evaluation scored every patient once. The three- and four-fold sensitivity evaluations reused the first partition seed and first training seed, yielding seven additional fits.

For held-out inference, we averaged responder-class sequence scores within each patient. The primary AUROC combines responder--nonresponder comparisons within each test fold. It gives each eligible pair equal weight and awards half credit for ties. We report the median and observed range across the ten five-fold evaluations. These are descriptive summaries of repeated evaluations of the same cohort. Pooled out-of-fold AUROC and average precision are available in the results data.

\subsection{Matched Sequence-Composition Baseline}

We computed 422 features per patient from eligible sequence records. These were the mean and standard deviation of sequence length, 20 normalized amino-acid frequencies, and 400 normalized adjacent-pair frequencies. A scaler and L2-regularized logistic regression (\texttt{liblinear}, $C=0.1$) were fitted using the training patients on each of the same partitions. Baseline and DeepTCR scores were assessed using the same within-fold patient-level AUROC.

\subsection{Earlier Fixed-Partition Evaluations}

Two earlier full five-fold evaluations used a common patient partition. Their intervals used 2,000 patient bootstrap resamples stratified by response class and fold, conditional on the fitted scores. For the second run, the fold-1 shuffled-label diagnostic retrained the model after permuting labels among training patients, then evaluated the same seven held-out patients.

\subsection{Exploratory Repertoire Clustering}

The original descriptive clustering used up to 30,000 archived records. It standardized normalized CDR3 amino-acid 3-mer frequencies and one-hot V/J features, projected them onto 128 principal components, and fit ten K-means clusters. We recomputed the response-group mean of the archived within-patient cluster shares. Labels were used to summarize the results after clustering. This descriptive analysis shares the cohort used in the supervised evaluation.

\subsection{Patient-Held-Out Model Performance}

Across ten five-fold evaluations, median within-fold DeepTCR AUROC was 0.456, with values from 0.298 to 0.579 (Figure~\ref{fig:repeat} and Table~\ref{tab:performance}). The composition baseline had median 0.386 across five partitions (range 0.368--0.456). The median matched DeepTCR-minus-baseline difference was 0.053 (range $-0.070$ to 0.175), and DeepTCR scored higher in eight of ten comparisons. The median absolute difference between the two DeepTCR fits on the same partition was 0.088 (range 0.070--0.140).

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Principal Findings}

The primary median DeepTCR AUROC was below 0.50, and individual evaluations ranged from 0.298 to 0.579. Although DeepTCR outscored the composition baseline on most matched evaluations, both median estimates fell below 0.50. The changes observed across training seeds and fold counts show how one estimate can give a different impression of performance in a small cohort.

\subsection{Interpretation in the Context of CAR-T Biomarkers}

Published studies have examined tumor burden, inflammatory markers and product phenotypes as indicators of CAR-T outcomes \citep{locke2020tumor,deng2020characteristics,fraietta2018determinants}. This study evaluated repertoire features within one cohort. It did not measure improvement over those clinical markers. The descriptive cluster shares provide a way to summarize repertoire variation without establishing predictive motifs or a clinical biomarker.

\subsection{Relationship to the Initial Analysis}

The January 2026 submission reported 0.754 AUROC from the first 100 logged test-epoch scores. Replaying its row-based split placed patients in both training and test sets, and the saved attention score array matched a seeded random fallback. The patient-held-out results and observed loss curves in this version replace those performance and attention findings.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Directions}

Only 34 patients were available. Repeated cross-validation changes the evaluation conditions while reusing the same people. Its range describes observed variation and does not estimate performance in a new clinical population. The training-label shuffle involved one small held-out fold and is a diagnostic. The exploratory clustering used a subset of the records and shares the supervised cohort. A separate cohort with compatible product sampling, sequencing and outcome definitions is needed to assess generalization.

\section{Conclusion}

Across 57 fits on patient-separated folds, the ten primary five-fold DeepTCR evaluations had median within-fold AUROC 0.456. The observed range and sensitivity checks show substantial variation. This study did not establish consistent patient-level discrimination of CD19 CAR-T response.
