\begin{abstract}
Can the endogenous T-cell receptor (TCR) repertoire in a CAR-T infusion product distinguish patients who respond to therapy three months later? We analyzed axicabtagene ciloleucel infusion products from 34 patients with large B-cell lymphoma, of whom 18 had a complete or partial response. We trained DeepTCR on beta-chain records and averaged the sequence scores for each held-out patient. Across five patient partitions and two training seeds, the median within-fold patient AUROC was \DeepMedian{} (range \DeepLow{}--\DeepHigh{}). A logistic regression on sequence length and amino-acid composition had a median AUROC of \BaseMedian{}. DeepTCR exceeded the matched baseline in \Above{} of ten evaluations. All evaluations used the same 34 patients. The median was close to chance and the result varied across patient partitions. These data do not establish predictive value in new patients.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Axicabtagene ciloleucel targets CD19 through an engineered receptor. The infused T cells also carry endogenous TCRs. \citet{li2023atlas} profiled individual cells from these infusion products and released the receptor sequences and clinical outcomes. This makes it possible to ask whether the distribution of endogenous receptors in a product contains information about subsequent response.

DeepTCR learns representations of receptor sequences and gene annotations \citep{sidhom2021deeptcr}. Here the outcome belongs to the patient. If records from one product enter both training and testing, a model can recognize that product without learning a pattern that transfers to another patient. We therefore held out entire products. We compared DeepTCR with a simpler model of repertoire composition and tested how the way chains are counted changes that baseline.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Patients, specimens and endpoint}
The source series GSE241783 contains 59 infusion-product libraries. We used the three-month PET/CT outcome in the publisher's Table S1A \citep{li2023atlas}. Fifteen patients had complete response, four had partial response and 16 had progressive disease. Five assessments were unevaluable and 19 were missing. One patient with complete response lacked deposited filtered TCR contigs. The study cohort therefore comprised 14 complete responders, four partial responders and 16 patients with progressive disease. The product was the specimen and the patient was the unit of evaluation.

We selected productive alpha and beta contigs and joined them by cell barcode within each product. The 198,267 cells with both chains yielded 239,637 alpha--beta pair rows. In 32,844 cells, multiple chains produced more than one pair. We retained this pair-row weighting for the primary analysis. Three pair rows had a beta CDR3 longer than 40 amino acids. The 239,634 remaining rows represented all 34 patients. An independent reconstruction from the GEO contigs reproduced every paired input row, including its multiplicity, and every outcome label.

\subsection{Classifiers and patient scores}
We used the large convolutional sequence classifier in DeepTCR\_SS 2.1.29. Its inputs were the beta CDR3 amino-acid sequence and its V and J genes. Constant alpha inputs satisfied the software interface. Within each training fold, unobserved gene categories mapped to the unknown category at inference. The two-class model trained for ten epochs with Adam, learning rate 0.001, minibatches of 1,024 and zero dropout. The training objective assigned each pair row its patient's outcome. For each held-out patient we averaged the responder-class scores over that patient's eligible rows.

The comparator used one feature vector per patient: the mean and standard deviation of CDR3 length, normalized frequencies for 20 amino acids and normalized frequencies for 400 adjacent amino-acid pairs. We standardized the 422 features on the training patients and fit an L2-regularized logistic regression ($C=0.1$, liblinear). Both models used identical training and test patients.

\subsection{Evaluation and controls}
We stratified patients by response in five independently seeded five-fold partitions. Two training seeds per partition gave ten evaluations. Each patient received one held-out score in every evaluation. One three-fold and one four-fold evaluation used the first partition seed and training seed as a sensitivity analysis. These designs required 57 fits. A separate fixed five-fold partition, one shuffled-training-label fit and an independent duplicate of a full fold brought the total to 64 ten-epoch fits.

The primary AUROC counts a concordant pair whenever a responder scores above a nonresponder held out from the same fitted model, with half credit for a tie. We summed concordances and divided by the total number of eligible pairs across the folds. Each five-fold evaluation had 57 such pairs. Pooling scores across different fitted models answers a different question, so we report the fixed partition's pooled AUROC separately. Ranges across partitions summarize variation within this cohort.

For the fixed partition, we bootstrapped held-out patients within fold and outcome class 2,000 times, keeping scores fixed. We also permuted test labels within folds 4,999 times as a fixed-score diagnostic. For the training-label control, we permuted labels among training patients in the first fixed fold and trained a new model for ten epochs. Its test outcomes were untouched. The graph and Keras initializers and minibatch ordering were seeded. An independent process refit one full primary fold to test numerical reproducibility.

\subsection{Counting chains and clustering repertoires}
The primary weighting can enter the same beta contig several times if it pairs with multiple alpha contigs. We refit the composition model using each productive beta contig once in cells with both chains. We also included productive beta contigs from cells without a productive alpha chain. Both checks retained the same patients, length limit, folds and regression settings.

For exploratory analyses, we sampled 50,000 and 30,000 pair rows approximately in proportion to product size. Within each analysis we standardized normalized CDR3 three-mer frequencies and V/J indicators before principal component analysis (PCA). The larger analysis retained 50 components and five K-means clusters. The smaller retained 128 components and ten clusters. To assess stability, we compared the fixed-seed and archived cluster assignments using the adjusted Rand index on matched rows and compared per-patient occupancy after matching cluster labels by minimum absolute occupancy difference. Cluster assignments used no outcome labels.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Patient-held-out discrimination}
DeepTCR's median within-fold AUROC across ten five-fold evaluations was \DeepMedian{} (\DeepLow{}--\DeepHigh{}). The composition baseline scored \BaseMedian{} (\BaseLow{}--\BaseHigh{}) across the five partitions. DeepTCR scored higher in \Above{} evaluations, lower in \Below{} and tied in \Ties{}. Table~\ref{tab:primary} shows both training seeds for each partition. In the three-fold evaluation DeepTCR scored \ThreeDeep{} and the baseline \ThreeBase{}. With four folds they scored \FourDeep{} and \FourBase{}, respectively (Figure~\ref{fig:primary}).

\subsection{Fixed-partition checks}
Across the separate five-fold partition, the model correctly ranked the equivalent of \FixedWins{} of \FixedPairs{} within-fold pairs (AUROC \FixedAUC{}, conditional 95\% bootstrap interval \CILow{}--\CIHigh{}). The pooled out-of-fold AUROC was \PooledAUC{} and average precision was \AvgPrecision{}. At a 0.5 threshold the model classified \NumCorrect{} of 34 patients correctly. Permuting the fixed test labels produced at least the observed concordance in \PermutationHits{} of 4,999 draws. In the training-label control, held-out AUROC was \ShuffleAUC{} compared with \ControlTrueAUC{} for the same test patients using the recorded training outcomes. These two control estimates come from one small test fold. Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:fixed} gives all five denominators.

In a separate process the first full fold of the primary design produced identical probabilities for all seven held-out patients. Repeated initializations at the same Keras and graph seed matched across all 19 trainable tensors. These checks concern computational repeatability on the tested CPU environment. An earlier run on the same partitions seeded the TensorFlow graph alone and yielded a median AUROC of 0.456 (range 0.298--0.579). Seeding the Keras initializers as well changed the fitted scores. Both estimates are close to chance.

\subsection{Effect of chain counting}
Giving one vote to each beta contig in the paired cells increased the baseline's AUROC in four of five partitions and left it equal in the fifth. The median rose from 0.386 to 0.421. Including beta-only cells yielded a median of 0.386. Table~\ref{tab:chains} gives the eligible counts and all five estimates. These comparisons apply to the composition model.

\subsection{Exploratory repertoire structure}
The first 50 components explained 3.44\% of the variance in the 50,000-row analysis. In the 30,000-row analysis, 128 components explained 7.69\%. In the ten-cluster analysis, the largest cluster contained a mean of 57.2\% of each responder's records and 59.0\% of each nonresponder's records. The same 50,000 input rows in the same order gave an adjusted Rand index of 0.444 between the fixed-seed five-cluster partition and the comparison partition from the trainee analysis. After matching the ten clusters in the smaller analysis, the mean absolute difference in occupancy was 5.51 percentage points across 34 patients and ten clusters. These comparisons limit how specifically an individual cluster can be interpreted.

\section{Discussion}
DeepTCR ranked patients more accurately than the composition model in \Above{} of ten comparisons, yet its median AUROC was close to chance. On the separate fixed partition the AUROC was \FixedAUC{}. With only six or seven test patients per fold, the ordering of a few patients can substantially change the estimate.

The distinction between a pair row and a patient matters twice. First, the classifier learns from more rows in patients with larger repertoires or cells with more chain pairings. Second, a held-out product supplies one outcome regardless of how many rows it contains. Within-fold AUROC preserves this distinction when assessing generalization across patients. The change in the baseline when beta contigs are counted once shows that a preprocessing decision can change patient rankings without changing the cohort.

The cohort contains 34 patients with known imaging outcomes from a single source study. Complete-case selection may limit how well these products represent all treated patients. The five primary partitions share patients, and the bootstrap interval conditions on already fitted predictions. Neither quantity measures performance at another center. The comparison also does not determine whether repertoires add information beyond clinical covariates or product phenotype. Testing that question requires an independent cohort, a specified assessment time and a prespecified unit for counting chains.
